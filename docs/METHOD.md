# Method

## Purpose and unit

The Pairwise Paper Evaluator is a structured workflow for comparing two manuscripts for a declared purpose, such as calibration against a fixed reference set or diagnosis of a revision. It does not produce an objective measure of scientific worth.

Each pairwise edge records the two neutral manuscript IDs, direction by rubric dimension, overall verdict, confidence, near-tie flag, decision hinge, a fact that could change the verdict, and exact evidence locators.

## Evidence cards

Read each manuscript once for the current task and freeze a concise evidence card before making pairwise judgments. Include the research question, contribution, data and evaluation setting, design or identification, uncertainty, validity concerns, strongest supported claim, claim boundary, practical consequence, main weakness, and precise locators. Use the card for comparisons, then reread only the cited passages needed to resolve a decision hinge.

## Scientific Validity Gate

Before an overall verdict, record the most important scientific-validity concern: identification, inference dependence, measurement, generalization, reproducibility, other, or none. Record severity as blocking, material, non-blocking, or none, and centrality as core, supporting, or peripheral. A blocking concern about a central conclusion cannot be compensated by novelty, scale, clarity, or appeal.

## Position reversal

Rejudge selected pairs with left/right order reversed while holding the rubric and evidence constant. Record the reversed judgment as a stability audit. It is not an additional vote and must not be counted twice in the aggregate.

## Local summaries

For a fixed reference set, a compact target summary may be computed as `(wins + 0.5 × ties) / number of original edges`. Report the underlying edges, confidence, and near-ties alongside it. This is a summary of that local set, not a universal latent-quality order, percentile, or acceptance probability.

## Blindness and version comparisons

Where practical, hide prior judgments, prior outcomes, author prestige, citation counts, publication status, and identity mappings. For a revision question, hold unchanged manuscript sections constant and compare only the section that changed.
